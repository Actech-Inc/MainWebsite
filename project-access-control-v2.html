Project Example / Access Control Hardware

Access control hardware

Developing Complete Hardware from a Hand Sketch

Starting with a hand sketch and broad functional requirements, Actech developed the mechanical concept and coordinated specialist suppliers to deliver assembled, tested and packaged hardware for the customer to configure with its own software.

Product
Fingerprint / RFID access control and attendance hardware
Starting point
A hand sketch and broad functional requirements
Actech’s scope
Mechanical design, sourcing, integration, assembly, testing and packaging
Design & delivery responsibilities
Specialist-designed industrial computer; customer-developed software; assembled hardware supplied by Actech

01 / THE STARTING POINT

Challenge

A plastic front-panel request became a broader hardware-development project, with the product definition and mechanical structure still to be resolved.

An undefined product concept

The original request concerned a plastic front panel, without a complete hardware specification.

Limited mechanical design resources

The customer needed help translating functional requirements into an appearance concept, component layout and manufacturable structure.

LCD fit and assembly constraints

The existing LCD specification and available assembly space required repeated adjustments to resolve interference between parts.

02 / OUR RESPONSE

Actech’s Approach

Actech defined the mechanical concept and coordinated the specialist contributions needed to deliver complete hardware.

Define the complete hardware

Actech proposed appearance concepts, clarified the requirements and developed the mechanical design and 3D CAD.

Coordinate specialist suppliers

The customer specified the fingerprint and RFID readers. Actech helped select other components, sourced an industrial computer supplier and served as a single point of contact.

Integrate and verify

The front panel, rear housing, LCD, fingerprint reader, RFID reader and industrial computer module were integrated through assembly, functional testing and packaging.

03 / PUTTING IT INTO PRACTICE

Engineering to Production

The design progressed through sample adjustments, a pilot build and production, followed by a change to rear-housing fabrication.

  1. 01

    Mechanical concept

    An injection-molded front panel and laser-cut sheet-metal rear housing balanced appearance with the initial tooling investment.

  2. 02

    Sample refinement

    Approximately 3–4 sample versions incorporated requirement details, LCD fit adjustments and findings from assembly.

  3. 03

    Pilot build and fixtures

    A pilot run preceded production. Actech made assembly and functional-test fixtures to check RFID, fingerprint, display and I/O.

  4. 04

    Production and optimization

    Hardware was assembled, functionally tested and packaged. Rear-housing fabrication later changed to NCT (CNC turret punching).

04 / WHAT CHANGED

Results & Impact

~8 months

From initial engagement to first production

500 units

First production batch

~50% lower rear-housing unit cost

After switching from laser cutting to NCT

This reduction applies to the rear housing, not to the complete product or total project cost.

Project context

The collaboration ran approximately from 2006 to 2024. Later batches were approximately 200–300 sets, each fully assembled, functionally tested and packaged before shipment.

Long-term value

An early concept became complete hardware, with mechanical adjustments supporting later changes to the host-computer specifications.

05 / KEY TAKEAWAY

From an idea to a complete product

Mechanical design, specialist sourcing and coordinated production turned an early concept into complete hardware for the customer’s own system.

Before you email

Which drawing formats should I send?

STEP (.step/.stp) or IGES (.iges/.igs) models, together with PDF drawings, are a useful starting point. Include dimensions, tolerances, materials and finishes. For other formats, email us first to confirm compatibility.

Can we arrange an NDA first?

Please email your NDA requirements before sending confidential drawings. The agreement and signing arrangements should be confirmed before you share sensitive project files.

How should I send large files?

Email a download link from your company-approved file-sharing service. Check access permissions and link expiry, or contact us first to agree on a suitable transfer method.

When can I expect an initial response?

Response timing depends on the scope and completeness of your request. Please include your required reply date and project deadline. An initial discussion and a completed technical quotation may require different timelines.

Should I provide order quantity or annual volume?

Please include both the initial order quantity and estimated annual demand, if known. Sample quantities, expected batch sizes and repeat-order frequency are also helpful. If volumes are uncertain, share your best estimate or the quantity ranges you would like quoted.
