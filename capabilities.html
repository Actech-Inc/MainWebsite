01 / Molded Plastic Components

Plastic injection molding

We coordinate injection molding for plastic housings and functional components, reviewing materials and production requirements for your application.

Typical parts & features
Housings and functional parts with features such as screw bosses, ribs, mating covers and molded-in metal inserts.
Materials
Common materials include ABS, PC, PC/ABS, Nylon (PA) and PMMA. Other materials are reviewed on request.
Engineering review
Review of part geometry, material selection, assembly fit, dimensional tolerances and appearance requirements.

Project experience

Our projects include housings for teaching equipment, handheld tools and electric-fence battery boxes, as well as small functional components. Assembly fit and structural strength are recurring priorities.

Design assessment

We review wall thickness, bosses and ribs, draft angles, mating features, surface textures and other drawing requirements. Mold-flow analysis is arranged where appropriate.

02 / Tooling for Production

Injection tooling

Our team works with tooling suppliers to plan injection molds around your part design, material requirements and expected production demand.

Tooling scope
Injection molds with features such as slides, lifters, interchangeable inserts and metal insert molding.
Tool planning
Mold steel, cavity layout and tool construction reviewed against part requirements and annual demand.
Timing & ownership
Customer-owned molds, with trial schedules and ongoing care agreed during project planning.
View technical details — Injection tooling+

Project experience

Our experience includes two- and three-plate molds, commonly with one or two cavities. We have also handled an eight-cavity mold for a part approximately 20 mm in size. Metal insert molding, including brass threaded inserts, is a frequent application. These examples describe past projects rather than limits on mold configuration.

Tooling assessment

Common mold steels include P20 and NAK80, with NAK80 often selected in our projects for higher appearance requirements. Cavity count is assessed against expected annual demand. Tooling specifications, ejector-pin locations and expected marks are confirmed with the customer before mold construction.

Schedule & care

The first mold trial (T1) typically takes place within 45–60 working days after receipt of the initial payment and confirmation of drawings and tooling specifications. Availability of specified molding materials may affect this schedule. Customer-owned molds are stored at the molding supplier. Storage and routine maintenance are generally provided at no additional charge; unusual damage and mold transfer arrangements are discussed separately.

Discuss your requirements

Share your part drawings, molding material, expected quantities and target schedule so we can review a suitable tooling approach. Two-shot and overmolding projects require a separate review of the proposed tool and production arrangement.

03 / Machined Metal Components

CNC machining

Actech brings metal machining and aluminum extrusion together, covering extrusion tooling, component production and surface finishing.

Typical components & features
Housings, rail components and machined parts with holes, threads and mating features.
Materials & machining
Common materials include 6061-T6 aluminum, 316 stainless steel and 6063 extrusions.
Dimensions & fit
Drawing-based review of dimensions and fit, with tolerance advice when mating parts are available.
Photo 1 of 5
View technical details — CNC machining & aluminum extrusion+

Project experience

Examples include cylindrical LED housings approximately 100 mm in diameter and 100 mm high, aluminum rulers 500 mm or 1,000 mm long, and other extruded components around 150 mm long. These are past project dimensions, not machining limits. LED housings emphasize appearance and finish consistency; rulers and rails emphasize dimensions and flatness.

Machining & finishing

Common operations include turning, milling, drilling, tapping and cutting to length. Aluminum extrusion projects can include tooling, extrusion production, secondary machining and surface finishing. Scale markings are applied by laser marking or screen printing.

Dimensional requirements

Customer drawings and specifications form the basis of our review. When mating components are available, we can assess fit and suggest tolerances for customer approval. Material grades, geometry and critical requirements are reviewed for each project.

Discuss your requirements

Share your drawings, material specification, quantities and critical dimensions so we can assess a suitable manufacturing approach. Include any mating components, flatness requirements and finish expectations relevant to the application.

04 / Appearance & Protection

Surface finishing

We arrange surface finishing for metal and plastic components, matching the process to the material, appearance requirements and intended use.

Processes & substrates
Options include blasting, anodizing, passivation, ABS chrome plating, printing and laser marking.
Appearance acceptance
Customer-approved reference samples establish the acceptable appearance range.
Checks & testing
Print adhesion checks, with supplier coating-thickness and corrosion testing arranged on request.
Photo 1 of 6
View technical details — Surface finishing+

Materials & processes

Our experience includes aluminum abrasive blasting and natural, black or commonly requested anodized colors; stainless steel passivation; metal laser marking; plastic pad and screen printing; and bright chrome plating on ABS. Aluminum rulers also use screen-printed scales. Powder coating and painting can be discussed as part of the project requirements.

Visual reference samples

We ask customers to provide samples defining the upper and lower appearance limits. If unavailable, samples are selected during subsequent production and submitted for customer approval. Uncertain cases are referred to the customer for sample confirmation.

Inspection scope

Actech checks printed markings by applying and removing tape to observe any lifting or removal. Coating-thickness and corrosion-resistance testing are arranged through suppliers when requested. Basic test data can be provided; formal test reports are not included.

Discuss your requirements

Share the substrate, finish, color and appearance requirements, together with reference samples where available. Specify any coating-thickness, color-matching or corrosion-resistance requirements so we can review the proposed finish and inspection scope.

05 / From Components to Finished Product

Assembly & PCBA integration

Our team manages component sourcing, assembly, testing and packing through suppliers to help turn your mechanical and electronic designs into finished products.

Integration scope
Plastic components, PCBA and hardware brought together through assembly and shipment packing.
Customer inputs
BOM and production data, plus firmware files and functional test procedures where required.
Testing & packing
Documented supplier instructions, followed by Actech checks of finished-product samples.
Photo 1 of 2
View technical details — Assembly & PCBA integration+

Production responsibilities

Actech coordinates component sourcing and documents assembly, testing and packing requirements for suppliers to follow. The scope can include mechanical and electronic assembly, individual boxing and carton packing. Operations and deliverables are agreed for each project.

Firmware & electronic testing

Firmware programming is arranged with subcontractors using customer-supplied files. Electronic functional testing is performed by the assembly supplier using customer-provided software and procedures. Circuit design and modification are not part of our current service scope.

Test scope

Without a specified test procedure, checks cover appearance and basic function for non-PCBA items, and continuity for PCBAs. Customer-specified tests are added to the agreed scope. Actech samples finished products before shipment to check function, appearance and packing.

Discuss your requirements

Share your BOM, assembly information, intended use and testing and packing requirements so we can define the integration scope. Waterproof sealing or controlled assembly environments require a separate feasibility review before commitment.

Before you email

Which drawing formats should I send?

STEP (.step/.stp) or IGES (.iges/.igs) models, together with PDF drawings, are a useful starting point. Include dimensions, tolerances, materials and finishes. For other formats, email us first to confirm compatibility.

Can we arrange an NDA first?

Please email your NDA requirements before sending confidential drawings. The agreement and signing arrangements should be confirmed before you share sensitive project files.

How should I send large files?

Email a download link from your company-approved file-sharing service. Check access permissions and link expiry, or contact us first to agree on a suitable transfer method.

When can I expect an initial response?

Response timing depends on the scope and completeness of your request. Please include your required reply date and project deadline. An initial discussion and a completed technical quotation may require different timelines.

Should I provide order quantity or annual volume?

Please include both the initial order quantity and estimated annual demand, if known. Sample quantities, expected batch sizes and repeat-order frequency are also helpful. If volumes are uncertain, share your best estimate or the quantity ranges you would like quoted.
