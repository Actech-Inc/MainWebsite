Project Example / Scientific Instrument

Digital photogate timer

Redesigning an Established Timer for Continued Production

To take over production of an established timer, Actech proposed an updated electronic architecture and developed the mechanical design to support production and assembly, reduce product cost and enable later technical improvements.

Product
Digital photogate timer for physics education and laboratory use
Starting point
An existing product and functional descriptions
Actech’s scope
Redesign proposal, mechanical development, specialist coordination, assembly and verification
Design & delivery responsibilities
Partner-developed electronics and firmware; finished hardware assembled, tested and packaged by Actech

01 / THE STARTING POINT

Challenge

The request was to take over production. Actech identified an opportunity to update the architecture while preserving the established functions and interface.

An aging electronic architecture

More than twenty TTL ICs brought cost, supply and component-obsolescence risks.

Incomplete engineering records

Functions needed to be established through testing the existing product and confirming its behavior with the customer.

An established user interface

The internal design needed updating while retaining the familiar front panel and its four operating modes.

02 / OUR RESPONSE

Actech’s Approach

Actech proposed the redesign, developed the mechanical structure and commissioned specialist electronic development.

Propose an updated architecture

Actech proposed replacing TTL hardware logic with a microcontroller and firmware.

Coordinate specialist development

A specialist partner developed the electronics, firmware and PCBA. Actech coordinated integration and handled mechanical development.

Confirm function through testing

Existing-product tests and customer confirmation established the four modes and informed the finished-product functional-test method.

03 / PUTTING IT INTO PRACTICE

Engineering to Production

Functional definition guided the electronic and mechanical redesign, sample preparation and verification of every finished unit.

  1. 01

    Functional definition

    Gate, Pulse, Pendulum and Stopwatch modes were confirmed against the behavior of the existing product.

  2. 02

    Electronic and mechanical redesign

    More than twenty TTL ICs were replaced by one main CPU and three auxiliary ICs, with firmware implementing the four modes. The PCBA comprised main, LCD and I/O boards. Housing changes improved screw fixing, assembly, component retention, wiring and the battery cover.

  3. 03

    Samples and manufacturing preparation

    Approximately 1–2 sample versions supported preparation of injection tooling and plastic parts, SMD/DIP assembly, component integration and wiring.

  4. 04

    Assembly and functional verification

    Actech assembled and packaged the finished hardware. A purpose-built fixture tested every unit’s four modes and two signal-input sockets before shipment.

04 / WHAT CHANGED

Results & Impact

~6 months

From initial engagement to first production

100% functional testing

Every unit tested before shipment

~50% lower product cost

Following the electronic and mechanical redesign

This reduction applies to product cost, not to an individual rear-housing component or total project expenditure.

Project context

Cost savings on the first production batch nearly offset development and tooling expenses. Historically reported annual sales were approximately 504 units; this is not a first-batch quantity or a statement of current sales.

Long-term value

In 2010, the specialist partner further updated the microcontroller, firmware and PCBA in response to a need for improved timing precision. Comparative verification records were not retained, so no quantified precision improvement is claimed.

05 / KEY TAKEAWAY

Redesigning for cost and product longevity

A production-transfer request became an opportunity to update the architecture, reduce product cost and support continued technical development.

Before you email

Which drawing formats should I send?

STEP (.step/.stp) or IGES (.iges/.igs) models, together with PDF drawings, are a useful starting point. Include dimensions, tolerances, materials and finishes. For other formats, email us first to confirm compatibility.

Can we arrange an NDA first?

Please email your NDA requirements before sending confidential drawings. The agreement and signing arrangements should be confirmed before you share sensitive project files.

How should I send large files?

Email a download link from your company-approved file-sharing service. Check access permissions and link expiry, or contact us first to agree on a suitable transfer method.

When can I expect an initial response?

Response timing depends on the scope and completeness of your request. Please include your required reply date and project deadline. An initial discussion and a completed technical quotation may require different timelines.

Should I provide order quantity or annual volume?

Please include both the initial order quantity and estimated annual demand, if known. Sample quantities, expected batch sizes and repeat-order frequency are also helpful. If volumes are uncertain, share your best estimate or the quantity ranges you would like quoted.
